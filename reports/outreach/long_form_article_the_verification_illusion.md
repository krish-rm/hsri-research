## 1. The Death of Human Oversight: The Verification Latency Wedge

The foundational lie of contemporary AI governance is the phrase **"Human-in-the-Loop" (HITL)**.

In European regulatory halls (EU AI Act Article 14) and American defense whitepapers alike, HITL is invoked as a talismanic defense against catastrophic runaway risk. Regulators reassure the public that an alert human operator will review, verify, and approve autonomous model decisions before they take effect.

Our empirical tracking proves that this concept has already become an administrative fiction.

We measure the **Verification Latency Wedge** ($\Delta T_{\text{wedge}}$): the mathematical divergence between the time required for an autonomous system to generate complex, mission-critical artifacts ($	au_{\text{gen}}$) and the time required for a certified human expert to rigorously audit that artifact for subtle, catastrophic defects ($	au_{\text{verify}}$):

$$\Delta T_{\text{wedge}} = 	au_{\text{verify}} - 	au_{\text{gen}}$$

Consider what has happened across six model generations:

## 3. The Negative Cross-Elasticity: Why More Compute Decreases Discernment

One of the most provocative discoveries formalized in HSRI 2.0 is the **Negative Cross-Elasticity of AI Infrastructure**:

$$\frac{\partial P_2}{\partial P_4} < 0 \quad \text{for } P_4 > P_4^*$$

Beyond a critical threshold of ambient AI integration ($P_4^*$), **each additional increase in autonomous AI efficiency degrades human critical discernment ($P_2$).**
