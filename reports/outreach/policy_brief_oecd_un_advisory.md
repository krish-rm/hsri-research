## 1. Executive Summary

Global multilateral composite indicators &mdash; including standard UNESCO, OECD, and World Bank indices &mdash; utilize arithmetic linear aggregation ($H = \sum w_i P_i$). This mathematical choice embeds an implicit and dangerous assumption: **perfect compensability**. Under linear aggregation, an economy with an extreme deficit in democratic oversight or cognitive verification can mathematically compensate by purchasing more servers, expanding fiber networks, or subsidizing model tokens.

The HSRI 2.0 framework establishes that **civilizational risk is strictly non-compensatory**.

By introducing the **Leontief Minimum Floor ($eta = 0.50$)** across 39 benchmark nations, HSRI 2.0 demonstrates that true preparedness is governed by an economy's weakest sovereign link. This mathematical formalization exposes a profound **Global Sovereignty Divide**:

1. **The High-Compute Vulnerability Trap:** Hyper-wealthy hubs (Singapore, Hong Kong, United States) experience massive rank collapses (-34, -19, -21 respectively) due to severe dependencies on closed foreign cloud APIs and low decoupling half-lives.
2. **The Sovereign Resilience Belt:** Balanced socio-technical democracies with high civic trust, independent legal verification, and decentralized infrastructure (Norway, Finland, Iceland, Denmark, Germany, Austria) represent the true frontier of superintelligence resilience.
3. **The Global South Asymmetry:** Emerging nations face dual disenfranchisement &mdash; lacking both sovereign compute and the institutional elasticity (P8) required to weather labor disruption.

1. **Adopt Non-Compensatory Benchmarking:** Replace purely linear scoring in future OECD and UN AI readiness frameworks with Leontief flooring or geometric mean formulations to highlight critical institutional bottlenecks.
2. **Include Post-Labor Institutional Elasticity (P8):** Multilateral agencies must track the fiscal capacity of member states to decouple tax revenue from human labor without sovereign debt default.
