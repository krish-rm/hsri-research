Why Coverage is Limited to 39 Nations in v0.2

The Humanity Superintelligence Readiness Index (HSRI) prioritizes epistemic integrity over artificial completeness. Generating headline scores for nations that lack validated empirical measurement would introduce severe automation bias, creating an illusion of precision that harms evidence-based policy.

1. The 60% Pillar Completeness Rule

To receive a composite headline score, a nation must possess observed empirical data for at least 60% of indicators within each of the four core pillars. While 86 evaluated economies have robust institutional governance tracking (e.g., Worldwide Governance Indicators) or telecommunications data (ITU), none possess empirical adult cognitive testing microdata.

2. The Structural NaN Policy

Non-participation in international testing programs like OECD PIAAC (Program for the International Assessment of Adult Competencies) or EU EMLI (European Media Literacy Index) is an administrative reality, not a random missing value. Imputing scores across non-participating nations would confound actual cognitive discernment with administrative affiliation.

3. Required Data to Expand Coverage

Expanding HSRI coverage beyond 39 nations requires non-OECD statistical agencies to administer standardized digital reasoning instruments, adult problem-solving assessments in technology-rich environments, and calibrated algorithmic trust surveys.
