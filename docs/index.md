## Executive Summary

The **Human Superintelligence Readiness Index (HSRI)** is a research feasibility inquiry assessing whether human psychological, cognitive, and behavioral readiness for increasingly capable artificial intelligence can be scientifically measured and indexed.

**Core Empirical Finding:** Human Superintelligence Readiness (HSR) is **not** a new psychological faculty. Every candidate sub-component—attentional control, metacognition, decision agency, value clarity, AI literacy, and calibrated trust—already possesses an established, validated literature base. The genuine, narrow research gap is whether these substrates, measured *jointly and specifically under interaction with an AI system whose task performance meets or exceeds the person's own*, predict outcomes that separate instruments do not already explain. That is a testable empirical hypothesis, not yet an established construct.
{: .evidence-strong }

**Headline Recommendation:** **DEVELOP AS A RESEARCH CONSTRUCT FIRST.** Not "develop now" (no validation data exists, and public indexing risks are unmanaged), and not "concept not sufficiently distinct" (a defensible, narrow integration gap exists once the construct is restricted to AI-interaction behavior).
{: .evidence-theoretical }

**Epistemic Standard:** Because this is a feasibility assessment about a proposed scientific construct, it holds itself strictly to scientific auditability:
- Every effect size, citation, and experimental parameter is either traceable to a verified peer-reviewed source or explicitly marked as illustrative or unverified (`[UNVERIFIED — NEEDS SOURCE]`).
- Spurious precision (e.g., invented decimal effect sizes or fabricated hallucination rates) has been deliberately removed.
- Claims are categorized into five transparent evidence tiers, with full audit trails cataloged in the [Master Evidence Table](https://github.com/krish-rm/hsri-research/blob/main/research/evidence/master-evidence-table.csv).

## Why Human Superintelligence Readiness Matters

For decades, international technology-readiness frameworks (such as network infrastructure indices, digital economy benchmarks, and government AI-readiness indices) have measured compute, bandwidth, and capital adoption, implicitly assuming that human agency remains sovereign and technology acts as a passive amplifier of human intent. As AI systems advance toward general-purpose reasoning, drafting, planning, and autonomous execution, that sovereignty assumption weakens in four specific, testable failure modes:

1. **Cognitive Offloading and Deskilling:** Well-established for memory and navigation tasks (transactive-memory effects with search engines and GPS navigation); the extension to active synthesis, argumentation, and code generation by generative AI is a live, active research question rather than a settled finding.
{: .evidence-preliminary }
2. **Automation Complacency Under Fluent Output:** Confident, fluent, syntactically correct AI output frequently appears more trustworthy than it is, triggering well-documented automation bias and reducing independent analytical verification.
{: .evidence-strong }
3. **Value and Goal Drift Under Optimization:** The hypothesis that AI's expanding capability to optimize means increases human reliance on explicit clarity about ends is philosophically coherent but currently empirically untested.
{: .evidence-theoretical }
4. **Trust Miscalibration in Both Directions:** Populations lacking calibrated trust either over-rely on automated advice (misuse) or reflexively reject valid machine assistance (disuse)—a dynamic documented across decades of automation literature predating modern generative models.
{: .evidence-strong }

Critically, investigating these failure modes does not require assuming that artificial general intelligence or superintelligence will emerge. It requires only that AI systems already outperform individual humans at specific, high-frequency cognitive tasks—a reality observable today in chess engines, diagnostic classifiers, forecasting models, and code-synthesis agents.
{: .evidence-strong }

## Epistemic Standards and Evidence Tiers

To allow rapid scrutiny by skeptical reviewers, claims across this repository are tagged using a five-tier taxonomy:

| Tier | Tag | Meaning & Standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: Strong** | `{: .evidence-strong }` | Multiple converging peer-reviewed sources; replicated empirical findings; multi-decade consensus literatures. |
| **Tier 2: Moderate** | `{: .evidence-moderate }` | Single high-quality randomized controlled trial (RCT), meta-analysis with caveats, or small consistent literature. |
| **Tier 3: Preliminary** | `{: .evidence-preliminary }` | Active, recent research area (e.g., 2020–2026 generative AI studies); findings replicate across limited contexts. |
| **Tier 4: Theoretical** | `{: .evidence-theoretical }` | Coherent logical, mathematical, or philosophical argument without direct empirical testing in an AI-specific setting. |
| **Tier 5: Speculative** | `{: .evidence-speculative }` | Plausible extrapolation or scenario framing; currently outside empirical reach. |
| **Unverified** | `[UNVERIFIED — NEEDS SOURCE]` | Claim appears in source materials without an identifiable, checked empirical citation. |

All empirical claims are indexed in the [Master Evidence Table](https://github.com/krish-rm/hsri-research/blob/main/research/evidence/master-evidence-table.csv), detailing claim text, page references, evidence tier, citations, and DOI/URL links.

## Repository and Report Structure

This documentation is structured into nine sequential analytical sections:

1. **[Definition and Scope](01-definition.md):** Operational definition, candidate definitions comparison, explicit boundary exclusions, and capability levels A through F.
2. **[Existing Global Indices](02-existing-indices.md):** Methodological review of HDI, SPI, World Happiness Report, MPI, GII/GKI, documented criticisms, and four architectural lessons for HSRI.
3. **[Construct Redundancy Audit](03-construct-audit.md):** Systematic mapping against validated psychological constructs, automation bias literature, cognitive forcing functions, and contemplative science.
4. **[Causal Model and Index Design](04-causal-model-and-index-design.md):** Human agency as the organizing axis, 4-tier conceptual scaffold, indicator retention decisions, and aggregation models (individual, organizational, national).
5. **[Measurement and Experiments](05-measurement-and-experiments.md):** Multi-method measurement strategy and nine standardized behavioral human-AI interaction experiments.
6. **[Cross-Cultural Validity and Ethics](06-cross-cultural-and-ethics.md):** Measurement invariance requirements, eight ethical failure modes (including responsibility laundering), and governance charters.
7. **[Objections and Counterarguments](07-objections.md):** Rigorous analysis of eleven major counterarguments, evaluated without defensive smoothing.
8. **[Research Roadmap](08-roadmap.md):** 11-phase research sequence (Phases 0–10), gating criteria, and the top ten open research gaps.
9. **[Final Recommendation](09-final-recommendation.md):** Core verdict, model selection, precise next pre-registered experiment, and reading list.
