# 1. Definition and Scope

A scientific index requires a falsifiable construct with explicit boundary conditions. Informal definitions of "AI readiness" frequently conflate inputs, cognitive processes, and normative outcomes into vague wellness or maturity labels that resist empirical disconfirmation. This section establishes the operational definition, evaluates alternative formulations, articulates explicit boundary exclusions, and specifies the capability framework within which the construct operates.

---

## 1.1 Candidate Definitions Compared

To clarify construct boundaries, four candidate formulations were systematically evaluated against psychometric criteria:

| Candidate Formulation | Conceptual Clarity | Construct Novelty | Measurability | Empirical Support | Cultural Neutrality | Pseudoscience Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. General maturity:** *"The general psychological and cognitive maturity of a person or society in the AI era."* | Low | Very Low (collapses into flourishing) | Low | N/A (too diffuse to test) | Low | High |
| **B. Future-event readiness:** *"A person's readiness for the eventual arrival of superintelligent AI."* | Low | Low | Very Low (predicated on an undefined future milestone) | None | Medium | High |
| **C. Input composite:** *"The composite of psychological stability, metacognition, agency, value clarity, and AI literacy that predicts preserved human agency when interacting with capable AI."* | Medium-High | Medium | Medium-High | Preliminary (component substrates validated separately) | Medium (requires cross-cultural testing) | Moderate (manageable with psychometric controls) |
| **D. Behavioral interaction (Recommended):** *"The demonstrated behavioral capacity to maintain calibrated trust, independent judgment, and value-directed goal-setting when interacting with AI systems whose task-specific performance meets or exceeds one's own."* | High | Medium-High (anchors to observable interaction rather than future forecasting) | High (operationalizable via behavioral performance tasks) | Preliminary but empirically testable today | Medium | Low (anchored in present, observable asymmetry) |

Candidate D avoids the definitional trap of predicting hypothetical technological milestones, instead isolating an observable psychological dynamic.
{: .evidence-theoretical }

---

## 1.2 Recommended Operational Definition

> **Human Superintelligence Readiness (HSRI Operational Definition):**  
> *The demonstrated behavioral capacity of an individual, organization, or society to maintain calibrated trust, independent judgment, and value-directed goal-setting when interacting with AI systems whose task-specific performance meets or exceeds their own &mdash; measured behaviorally wherever possible, not merely self-reported.*
{: .evidence-theoretical }

This definition deliberately anchors "readiness" to present-day, observable cognitive asymmetry (such as an individual interacting with a chess engine, diagnostic classifier, forecasting system, or code-generation agent) rather than an unproven future technological event. The term "superintelligence" is retained in the project's public title strictly as an aspirational scenario framing device, not as an empirical precondition.
{: .evidence-theoretical }

## 1.3 Explicit Boundary Exclusions

To prevent construct creep, HSRI explicitly excludes six commonly confused concepts:

| Excluded Concept | Common Misconception | Methodological Reason for Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| **General Intelligence (IQ)** | Assumed that higher cognitive ability guarantees immune response to AI error. | High cognitive ability does not prevent automation bias; fluent reasoners can rationalize erroneous machine outputs just as fluently as correct outputs. |
| **AI Literacy & Technical Skill** | Equated with psychological readiness and discernment. | Understanding model architectures or prompt engineering does not confer psychological immunity to persuasion, sycophancy, or over-reliance; technical literacy is a necessary but insufficient substrate. |
| **General Mental Health & Happiness** | Equated with flourishing and cognitive sovereignty. | An emotionally stable, satisfied individual can readily surrender decisional autonomy to an agreeable, highly capable AI assistant. |
| **Technological Infrastructure** | Equated with compute availability, broadband speed, and device access. | Measures physical capital and institutional adoption, which are distinct from individual cognitive discernment and decisional agency. |
| **Belief in Superintelligence** | Equated with being "prepared" for advanced AI. | Readiness is measured against observable task performance; ideological conviction or fear regarding hypothetical future AGI introduces confounding variance rather than diagnostic signal. |
| **Contemplative/Meditation Frequency** | Equated with attentional readiness and mindfulness. | Meditation is at most a candidate *training intervention* for an underlying substrate (executive attention); treating a specific behavioral practice as an outcome metric constitutes a category error. |

## 1.4 The Capability-Level Framework

To establish empirical scope boundaries, human-AI interaction is classified into six ascending capability tiers:

| Capability Tier | System Operational Role | Human Capabilities That Gain Marginal Value | Human Capabilities That Lose Marginal Value | Primary Behavioral Failure Mode | Empirical Feasibility Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Level A: Tool** | Executes deterministic, explicit human instructions. | Basic digital literacy, instruction syntax. | Rote manual calculation. | Non-adoption. | Established / Historical. |
| **Level B: Cognitive Assistant** | Drafts, summarizes, searches, and retrieves information. | Verification habits, prompt framing, source checking. | Manual information retrieval and preliminary formatting. | Mild over-reliance on conversational convenience. | Established / Current. |
| **Level C: Capable Collaborator** | Formulates strategies, options, syntheses, and persuasive arguments. | Discernment of subtle error, calibrated trust, value clarity. | Initial draft synthesis and baseline solution ideation. | Automation complacency; susceptibility to sycophantic framing. | Active Empirical Focus (HSRI Core). |
| **Level D: Autonomous Task Manager** | Executes multi-step workflows with intermittent supervision. | Oversight design, delegation judgment, accountability retention. | Manual execution of routine intermediate processes. | Deskilling; diffusion of decisional responsibility. | Active Empirical Focus (HSRI Core). |
| **Level E: Exceeds Human Reasoning Across Many Domains** | Consistently outperforms most individuals across most cognitive tasks. | Meta-evaluative judgment, value governance, epistemic humility, multi-agent coordination. | Individual domain expertise as a primary status marker. | Identity disruption; systemic epistemic dependence. | Preliminary / Cautious Extrapolation. |
| **Level F: Hypothetical Superintelligence** | Radically exceeds collective human cognitive capacity. | Unknown; likely requires institutional and societal governance mechanisms beyond individual psychology. | Individual cognitive competition. | Total cognitive obsolescence or loss of control. | Speculative (Outside Current Measurement). |

HSRI can be **empirically grounded through Level D and cautiously extrapolated to Level E**. Level F is retained exclusively as an orienting conceptual horizon, entirely outside what can be measured or validated using contemporary behavioral instruments.
{: .evidence-theoretical }
