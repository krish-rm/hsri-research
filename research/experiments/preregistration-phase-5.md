## 1. Study Overview & Institutional Mission

The Human Superintelligence Readiness Index (HSRI) investigates human cognitive resilience, agency retention, and institutional readiness as frontier artificial intelligence approaches radical capability asymmetry and superintelligence (ASI). A fatal psychometric vulnerability of technology readiness frameworks is over-reliance on self-reported competence and subjective trust scales, which are severely contaminated by social desirability, Dunning-Kruger effects, and automation complacency.

Phase 5 of the HSRI Scientific Roadmap establishes an empirical, multi-domain standardized behavioral battery. This protocol preregisters the methodology, experimental manipulation, stimulus calibration, and statistical estimation models for the complete suite of nine behavioral experiments:
1. **EXP-01 (Legal Domain):** Fluent hallucination detection in AI-drafted legal briefs.
2. **EXP-02 (Medical Domain):** Fluent hallucination detection in AI clinical summaries.
3. **EXP-03 (Technical/Code Domain):** Fluent hallucination detection in AI architectural documentation.
4. **EXP-04 (Financial/Quantitative Domain):** Fluent hallucination detection in AI corporate valuation and accounting models.
5. **EXP-05 (Choice-Overload Stress Test):** Decision paralysis and susceptibility to machine default anchors under high option density and time pressure.
6. **EXP-06 (Autonomous Delegation Offer):** Delegation propensity, cognitive offloading comprehension penalties, and post-hoc accountability attribution during 10-step workflows.
7. **EXP-07 (Belief-Challenge & Updating):** Bayesian belief updating versus dogmatic entrenchment, base rate neglect, and resistance to confirmation bias manipulation.
8. **EXP-08 (Demonstrated Machine Superiority):** Human self-efficacy preservation, independent hypothesis generation, and resilience to silent-machine paralysis following demonstrated capability asymmetry.
9. **EXP-09 (Cognitive-Forcing-Function Interface):** Replication and extension of Buçinca et al. (2021) evaluating unassisted, standard frictionless, and precommitment-forcing interaction modes.

## 2. Theoretical Framework & Confirmatory Hypotheses

### 2.1 Theoretical Framework
Human interaction with frontier AI is characterized by two distinct systemic risks:
1. **The Verification Latency Wedge ($	au_{	ext{gen}} \ll 	au_{	ext{verify}}$):** As AI generation speed accelerates to milliseconds while human cognitive verification requires minutes or hours, screen-based human oversight collapses into superficial rubber-stamping.
2. **The Asymmetry-Induced Agency Collapse:** When machine systems demonstrate visible superiority across analytical tasks, humans exhibit psychological surrender, suppressing independent hypothesis generation and deferring normative bioethical choices to statistical algorithms.

### 2.2 Suite of Confirmatory Hypotheses (H1 to H8)

- **Hypothesis 1 (H1: Verification Latency Wedge):**  
  Fluent AI-generated text containing embedded errors suppresses verification latency compared to disfluent control passages ($\Delta t < 0$, $p < .001$), predicting a corresponding reduction in error detection accuracy ($r_{latency, accuracy} > 0$).
- **Hypothesis 2 (H2: Domain Error Discrimination Stability):**  
  Across all knowledge domains (EXP-01 to EXP-04), stimulus item discrimination satisfies $D \ge 0.30$, with error detection positively predicted by cognitive reflection (CRT-2 score; $eta_{CRT} > 0$, $p < .01$).
- **Hypothesis 3 (H3: Quantitative Automation Deference):**  
  Participants evaluating quantitative and code domains (EXP-03, EXP-04) demonstrate significantly lower detection odds than in qualitative domains (EXP-01, EXP-02) when controlling for domain experience ($	ext{Odds Ratio} < 0.70$, $p < .01$).
- **Hypothesis 4 (H4: Machine Default Anchor Deference under Overload; EXP-05):**  
  In 8-option choice environments under 45-second deadlines, participant adoption of the AI top-ranked default exceeds baseline ($P(	ext{Default}) > 0.65$, $p < .001$), with decision timeouts predicted by Intolerance of Uncertainty (IUS-12; $eta_{IUS} > 0$, $p < .01$).
- **Hypothesis 5 (H5: Autonomous Delegation & Offloading Penalty; EXP-06):**  
  Participants who delegate full autonomy at Step 4 demonstrate lower post-task mechanistic comprehension ($\Delta 	ext{Comprehension} < -0.80$, $p < .001$) and lower Step 8 error interception ($	ext{OR} < 0.25$, $p < .001$) compared to manual supervisors, exhibiting increased algorithmic scapegoating.
- **Hypothesis 6 (H6: Empirical Belief Updating vs Entrenchment; EXP-07):**  
  Participants presented with valid counter-intuitive evidence update beliefs significantly less than Bayesian optimal models ($\Delta B < \Delta B_{Bayes}$, $p < .001$), while displaying heightened vulnerability to cherry-picked confirmation bias manipulation.
- **Hypothesis 7 (H7: Demonstrated Superiority & Agency Collapse; EXP-08):**  
  Experiencing demonstrated machine superiority in Phase I significantly depresses subsequent independent hypothesis generation in collaborative Phase II ($\Delta 	ext{Hypotheses} < -50\%$, $p < .001$), while increasing silent-machine paralysis latency.
- **Hypothesis 8 (H8: Cognitive Forcing Function Advantage; EXP-09):**  
  The cognitive forcing function interface (mandatory precommitment) significantly increases error override accuracy compared to frictionless standard assistance ($	ext{OR} > 2.50$, $p < .001$), eliminating the explanation placebo effect.

## 3. Experimental Design & Sampling Plan

### 3.1 Design Structure
- **Part I (EXP-01 to EXP-04):** 4 Between-Subjects Domains $	imes$ 2 Within-Subjects Accuracy Conditions (Calibrated Error vs Control).
- **Part II (EXP-05):** 2 Choice Density (High [8] vs Low [3]) $	imes$ 2 Machine Ranking (Default vs Unranked) Factorial Design.
- **Part III (EXP-06):** 10-Step Sequential Workflow with Step 4 Delegation Offer and Step 8 Injected Fault.
- **Part IV (EXP-07):** Pre/Post Belief Elicitation under Counter-Intuitive vs Spurious Counter-Evidence.
- **Part V (EXP-08):** Asymmetry Induction (Human vs AI Benchmarks) followed by Collaborative Synthesis with Silent-Machine Probes.
- **Part VI (EXP-09):** 3-Interface Randomized Trial: Unassisted vs Standard AI Assistance vs Cognitive Forcing Function.

### 3.2 Participant Sampling & Statistical Power Analysis
- **Target Population:** Educated adults (undergraduate or graduate degree, or current university enrollment) with foundational literacy and numeracy.
- **Sample Size:** $N = 120$ participants per protocol across 9 experimental protocols ($N_{total} = \mathbf{1,080}$ human participants).

## 5. Master Variables & Measurement Suite

### 5.1 Primary Behavioral Outcomes
1. **Error Detection Score ($0, 1, 2$):** Accuracy and mechanistic attribution across EXP-01 to EXP-04.
2. **Default Adoption Rate ($P(	ext{Default})$):** Propensity to select the machine top-ranked option under overload (EXP-05).
3. **Delegation Acceptance Rate ($P(	ext{Delegate})$):** Acceptance of autonomous execution at Step 4 (EXP-06).
4. **Step 8 Error Interception ($0$ vs $1$):** Overriding catastrophic machine faults before final commit (EXP-06).
5. **Mechanistic Comprehension Score ($0, 1, 2$):** Post-execution situational awareness (EXP-06).
6. **Belief Update Magnitude ($|P_1 - P_0|$):** Observed shift vs. normative Bayesian update (EXP-07).
7. **Independent Hypothesis Count:** Number and depth of valid alternatives generated (EXP-08).
8. **Appropriate Reliance Ratio ($RR$):** Ratio of following correct advice vs. rejecting flawed advice (EXP-09).

### 5.2 Cognitive Covariates & Individual Differences
- **Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT-2; Thomson & Oppenheimer, 2016):** 4-item reflective vs intuitive thinking scale.
- **Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale (IUS-12; Carleton et al., 2007):** Uncertainty tolerance measure.
- **Need for Cognition (NFC Short Form; Cacioppo et al., 1984):** Intrinsic motivation for deliberative thought.
- **Post-Hoc Accountability Attribution (5-Point Scale):** Human responsibility versus algorithmic scapegoating.

## 6. Confirmatory Statistical Models

1. **Generalized Linear Mixed-Effects Model (GLMM for EXP-01 to EXP-04):**
   $$\text{logit}(P(Y_{ij} = 1)) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{Domain}_j + \beta_2 \log(T_{ij}) + \beta_3 \text{CRT}_i + u_i + v_j + \epsilon_{ij}$$
2. **Binary Logistic Choice Regression (EXP-05):**
   $$\text{logit}(P(\text{SelectDefault}_i = 1)) = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 \text{ChoiceDensity}_i + \alpha_2 \text{IUS}_i + \alpha_3 \text{CRT}_i + \epsilon_i$$
3. **Cognitive Offloading Linear Regression (EXP-06):**
   $$\text{Comprehension}_i = \gamma_0 + \gamma_1 \text{Delegated}_i + \gamma_2 \text{CRT}_i + \epsilon_i$$
4. **Bayesian Belief Calibration Regression (EXP-07):**
   $$|P_{1,i} - P_{\text{Bayes},i}| = \lambda_0 + \lambda_1 \text{EvidenceType}_i + \lambda_2 \text{Dogmatism}_i + \epsilon_i$$
5. **Agency Preservation Poisson Regression (EXP-08):**
   $$\log(\mathbb{E}[\text{Hypotheses}_i]) = \theta_0 + \theta_1 \text{AsymmetryInduction}_i + \theta_2 \text{GeneralSelfEfficacy}_i + \epsilon_i$$
6. **Interface Interaction GLMM (EXP-09):**
   $$\text{logit}(P(\text{Override}_i = 1 | \text{AI Incorrect})) = \delta_0 + \delta_1 \text{Interface}_i + \delta_2 \text{NFC}_i + \epsilon_i$$
