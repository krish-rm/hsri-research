## Study Title
Human Discernment Under Cognitive Automation Pressure: Fluent Hallucination Detection in Clinical and Medical Advice (HSRI EXP-02)

---

## 1. Full Explanation of Study Purpose
Thank you for participating in this research study!

When you enrolled, you were informed that you would evaluate "AI-generated informational documents for clarity, tone, and quality." We would now like to provide a full and comprehensive explanation of the true study objectives, design, and scientific goals.

### The Phenomenon Under Investigation: Automation Bias & Medical Hallucinations
This experiment investigates **clinical automation bias**—the human cognitive vulnerability wherein individuals uncritically trust and accept advice produced by fluent, authoritative artificial intelligence models, even when that advice contains dangerous medical misinformation or contraindicated guidance.

In order to rigorously measure human discernment under cognitive automation pressure, our research team embedded one specific, calibrated factual distortion into each of the three medical advisory passages you reviewed.

---

## 2. Incomplete Disclosure and Deception Rationale
### Why Incomplete Disclosure Was Necessary
To observe natural cognitive discernment and evaluate baseline scrutiny without artificial cueing, participants could not be warned in advance that specific medical errors were intentionally embedded in the texts. Forewarning participants would induce an artificial proofreading posture ("demand characteristics"), rendering it impossible to measure how real users interact with fluent AI health advice in everyday contexts.

---

## 3. Explicit Medical Corrections for All Study Stimuli

> **CRITICAL MEDICAL DISCLAIMER & INJUNCTION**
> The passages presented during this study contained **deliberately fabricated and medically dangerous errors** designed solely for experimental testing. 
> **NONE of the medical advice, dosages, or treatment recommendations presented in the experimental stimuli should ever be used or relied upon in real life.** 
> If you have questions about your personal health, medications, or any medical condition, **always consult a qualified, licensed healthcare professional or clinician.**

Below are the exact corrections for each experimental passage you encountered:

### Item 1: Acetaminophen (Paracetamol) Dosing for Acute Musculoskeletal Pain
* **What the Stimulus Stated:** The passage advised taking an acute single dose of **5,000 mg** of acetaminophen for acute lumbar strain.
* **The Accurate Medical Fact:** 
  - The maximum safe adult daily dose of acetaminophen from **all sources combined** is **4,000 mg in 24 hours** (and many guidelines recommend limiting to 3,000 mg/day for chronic use or in older adults).
  - A single acute dose of 5,000 mg exceeds the total daily maximum and poses an acute risk of severe **hepatotoxicity (irreversible liver necrosis and acute liver failure)**.
  - Therapeutic adult dosing is typically 500 mg to 1,000 mg every 4 to 6 hours as needed, strictly not exceeding 4,000 mg in a 24-hour window.

### Item 2: Management of Acute Gastroenteritis and Fluid Balance
* **What the Stimulus Stated:** The passage recommended completely withholding all fluids for 24 hours during acute vomiting and diarrhea to "rest the gastrointestinal tract."
* **The Accurate Medical Fact:**
  - Withholding oral fluids during severe acute gastroenteritis accelerates acute dehydration, electrolyte derangement, hypovolemic shock, and acute prerenal kidney injury.
  - Clinical standards mandate early and frequent oral rehydration therapy (ORT) using balanced Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) containing appropriate sodium, potassium, and glucose ratios, or urgent intravenous fluid administration if oral intake is impossible. Fluids should **never** be withheld.

### Item 3: Ocular Trauma, Red Eye, and Topical Corticosteroid Drops
* **What the Stimulus Stated:** The passage recommended using leftover prescription fluorometholone (corticosteroid) eye drops for a painful red eye following foreign-body scratch/trauma without an in-person eye examination.
* **The Accurate Medical Fact:**
  - Applying topical ophthalmic corticosteroids to an unexamined red eye, corneal abrasion, or unknown infection is **strictly contraindicated**.
  - In the presence of a corneal scratch, fungal infection, or Herpes Simplex virus (HSV) epithelial keratitis, corticosteroids suppress the local immune response, accelerating ulceration, corneal melting, globe perforation, and irreversible vision loss.
  - Eye injuries and acute ocular symptoms require immediate evaluation by an optometrist, ophthalmologist, or emergency clinician using slit-lamp biomicroscopy and fluorescein staining prior to starting any drop therapy.
