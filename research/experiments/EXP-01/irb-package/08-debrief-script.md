# Participant Debriefing Form & Script

## Study Title
Human Discernment Under Cognitive Automation Pressure: Fluent Hallucination Detection in Legal Documentation (HSRI EXP-01)

## Full Explanation of Study Purpose
Thank you for participating in this research study!

When you began the study, you were informed that you would evaluate "AI-generated documents for quality." We would now like to provide a full explanation of the study design and scientific goals.

### The Phenomenon Under Investigation: Automation Bias & Fluent Hallucinations
This experiment investigates **automation bias**—the psychological tendency for individuals to uncritically trust and overlook errors in texts produced by automated artificial intelligence systems when those texts are grammatically fluent and authoritative.

In each of the three legal passages you read, our research team embedded one specific, calibrated error:
1. **Passage 1:** Claimed that a pre-injury exculpatory waiver absolved a defendant of gross negligence (legally and logically impossible, as waivers cannot waive gross negligence).
2. **Passage 2:** Stated that common carrier duty of care stems from the 14th Amendment of the U.S. Constitution (in fact, it stems from state-level common law tort doctrine).
3. **Passage 3:** Attributed municipal zoning and qualified immunity rules to *Brown v. Board of Education* (1954), which is actually a landmark public school racial desegregation decision.

### Why Was Incomplete Disclosure Necessary?
In order to obtain valid scientific measurements of natural cognitive discernment, it was essential that participants were not informed in advance about the presence or specific nature of the embedded errors. If participants had been told exactly what errors to search for, their reading behavior would have been altered by demand characteristics rather than reflecting real-world cognitive scrutiny.
