## 1. Epistemic Grounding & The Single-Model Vulnerability Mandate

A fatal weakness of automated AI safety evaluations and governance frameworks is **single-provider epistemic monoculture**: relying on outputs, verdicts, or safety evaluations generated by a single corporate model family (e.g., exclusively OpenAI or exclusively Google). Single-model pipelines inherit provider-specific alignment biases, RLHF sycophancy, commercial incentives, and cultural blind spots (Du et al., 2023; Liang et al., 2023; Chan et al., 2023).

Under Section 5.1 and Section 8.1 of the HSRI Scientific Architecture, Phase 9 establishes an **adversarial multi-agent deliberative ensemble** across seven independent global frontier LLM families, formalizing mathematical consensus thresholds before any methodology diff, proxy imputation, or index reweighting can be ratified.

## 3. Mathematical Consensus and Dispersion Metrics

### 3.1 Fleiss' Kappa for Multi-Rater Categorical Agreement
To quantify inter-model concordance across categorical debate verdicts (APPROVE, REJECT, MODIFY, CONTESTED), Phase 9 computes **Fleiss' Kappa ($\kappa_{\text{Fleiss}}$)** across $N$ debate topics rated by $n = 7$ models:
$$\kappa_{\text{Fleiss}} = \frac{\bar{P} - \bar{P}_e}{1 - \bar{P}_e}$$

Where:
- $\bar{P}$ is the observed mean proportion of agreeing model pairs.
- $\bar{P}_e$ is the expected agreement by chance.
- **Landis & Koch (1977) Benchmark Targets:**
  - $\kappa \ge 0.60$: Substantial Agreement (Minimum requirement for methodological axioms).
  - $\kappa \ge 0.80$: Near-Perfect Agreement.

### 3.2 Herfindahl-Hirschman Provider Concentration Index (HHI)
To guarantee structural antitrust and anti-monopoly dispersion in governance decisioning:
$$\text{HHI} = \sum_{i=1}^{K} s_i^2$$
Where $s_i = 1/7$ is the vote share of family $i$.
$$\text{HHI}_{\text{ensemble}} = 7 \times \left(\frac{1}{7}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{7} \approx 0.1429$$
- **Regulatory Standard:** $\text{HHI} \le 0.180$ (Department of Justice & FTC threshold for unconcentrated governance markets).

### 3.3 Shannon Cross-Provider Divergence Entropy ($H_{\text{div}}$)
For each topic $j$ with verdict probability distribution $p_k$:
$$H_{\text{div}}(j) = -\sum_{k=1}^{M} p_k \log_2(p_k)$$
- High entropy ($H > 1.2$) signifies active ideological and methodological contention requiring structured human review board arbitration.

## 4. Formal Deliberative Ratification Decision Rules

```
                      [ Topic Submitted to Ensemble ]
                                     │
                 ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
                 ▼                                       ▼
        [ Methodological Axiom ]             [ Policy / Empirical Proxy ]
                 │                                       │
        Is Unanimous (7/7)?                     Is Supermajority (≥ 5/7)?
           │           │                            │           │
          YES          NO                          YES          NO
           │           │                            │           │
           ▼           ▼                            ▼           ▼
      [ RATIFIED ] [ REJECTED /                 [ RATIFIED ] [ CONTESTED ]
                   REFERRED TO PI ]                          (Referred to Human
                                                              Review Board)
```

1. **Unanimous Consensus ($7/7$ or $100\%$):** Required to amend foundational constitutional rules (non-compensatory scoring, zero-PII privacy architecture).
2. **Supermajority Consensus ($\ge 5/7$ or $71.4\%$):** Required to approve proxy substitutions, pillar reweighting proposals, or country profile status transitions.
3. **Contested Split ($< 5/7$):** The topic is logged to `model-divergence-log.csv` as `CONTESTED`. No automated code/data changes may execute without explicit Principal Investigator (PI) review board arbitration.
