### 4.1 What the Framework Measures
- **Macro-Level Institutional & Survey Proxies:** The prototype harmonizes 780 national observations across 39 rated economies (completeness = 96.5%) into four equally weighted (25% each) pillars:
  - *AI Literacy & Skills:* Tertiary STEM graduation rates, youth/adult basic digital skills, and standardized reading comprehension.
  - *Critical Discernment:* Societal epistemic resilience against misinformation, media literacy index scores, and news verification awareness.
  - *Institutional Governance:* Administrative state capacity, legal frameworks, regulatory quality, and statutory data protections.
  - *Digital Infrastructure:* Telecommunications penetration, broadband bandwidth, and secure internet server density.
- **Structural Missingness Integrity:** Preserves structural NaNs (Rule 2) for un-surveyed economies (e.g., EMLI non-European economies; PIAAC PSTRE non-participating economies) and categorizes exactly 86 developing economies as unrated without extrapolation (Rule 3).

### 4.2 What the Framework Does NOT Measure
- **Individual Cognitive Discernment:** Does not measure human error detection, reading latency, or automation complacency under generative AI interaction.
- **Psychometric Validation:** Has not undergone structural equation modeling (SEM), confirmatory factor analysis, or item response theory (IRT) calibration on human populations.
- **National Capability Rankings:** Does not constitute an authoritative ranking or regulatory certification; it is an exploratory diagnostic prototype (`v0.3-dev`).
- **Frontier / Superhuman Preparedness:** Measures standard contemporary socioeconomic proxies; it does not measure resilience against radically superhuman, agentic, or self-improving AI systems.

## 7. Epistemic Separation of Claims (Evidence vs. Inference vs. Hypothesis vs. Speculation)

To prevent epistemic conflation, all substantive claims across the HSRI repository are separated into four distinct epistemic categories. Any unlocated claim is tagged `UNLOCATED`.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    EPISTEMIC SEPARATION TAXONOMY                                   |
+-------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Category          | Epistemic Standard & Definition                                                |
+-------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Evidence       | Direct empirical observations, verified dataset values, recorded logs          |
| 2. Inference      | Deductions derived logically from empirical data or formal modeling            |
| 3. Hypothesis     | Falsifiable propositions structured for future experimental testing            |
| 4. Speculation    | Conceptual extrapolations beyond observable ranges without empirical anchor    |
| 5. Unlocated      | Claims asserted about HSRI that cannot be located in repository files          |
+-------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 7.1 Category 1: Evidence (What the Repository Shows)
1. The harmonized dataset contains exactly 780 national observations across 39 rated economies and 4 pillars (`data/raw_observations_harmonized.csv`).
2. Exactly 86 economies tracked in international registries lack qualifying multi-pillar data and are classified as unrated (`data/unrated-nations.csv`).
3. EMLI surveys 41 European nations; all 30 European economies in HSRI have observed EMLI scores, while all 9 non-European economies are structural NaNs.
4. In OECD PIAAC Cycle 1, Cyprus opted out of PSTRE, and Bulgaria, Iceland, Malta, North Macedonia, and Romania did not participate in reported rounds; all 6 are preserved as structural NaNs in HSRI.
5. In synthetic persona pilots ($N=15$ per experiment), EXP-01 items demonstrated discrimination $D \in [0.72, 0.84]$; EXP-02 $D \in [0.61, 0.87]$; EXP-03 $D \in [0.67, 1.00]$.
6. Multi-agent debate executions to date have run exclusively against Google Gemini 3.8 Flash (`hsri_agents/logs/model-divergence-log.csv`).
7. **Zero human-participant behavioral data has been collected or validated in this repository.**

### 7.2 Category 2: Inferences (What the Repository Deduces)
1. Applying equal weighting (25/25/25/25) across pillars is a conservative baseline that prevents premature weight optimization in the absence of human factor analysis.
2. Naive mean imputation creates severe rank and band distortions (e.g., imputing Singapore's missing EMLI score drops it from Band A to Band B, $\Delta = 2.93$).
3. Synthetic LLM persona discrimination metrics ($D$) reflect prompt engineering and training distribution artifacts; they do not generalize to human cognitive performance.
4. Providing local machine explanations does not reliably eliminate automation bias and can induce an illusion of explanatory depth (Bansal et al., 2021; Buçinca et al., 2021).

### 7.3 Category 3: Hypotheses (What the Repository Proposes to Test)
1. *Joint Interaction Hypothesis:* An integrated behavioral battery measuring discernment under direct AI asymmetry explains significant incremental variance ($\Delta R^2 > 0$) over a linear composite of standalone instruments (CRT + CIHS + ANT + AI Literacy) (`research/outreach/01-redundancy-audit-post.md`).
2. *Framing Effect Hypothesis (EXP-01 RQ1):* Explicit labeling of text as "AI-generated" induces significantly greater automation bias (lower error detection rates, $d \ge 0.35$) compared to identical text labeled as "peer-reviewed human draft".
3. *Processing Latency Hypothesis (EXP-01 RQ2):* Longer inspection latency correlates positively with error detection accuracy, verifying deliberative System 2 engagement.
4. *Dosage Narrowing Hypothesis (EXP-02 Item 3):* Narrowing acetaminophen dosage from 5,000 mg to 4,200 mg QID will significantly reduce lay detection rates while maintaining clinical contraindication.

### 7.4 Category 4: Speculations (What the Repository Extrapolates)
1. *Capability Level F (Hypothetical Superintelligence):* The projection that radically superhuman AI will cause "total cognitive obsolescence or loss of control" (`docs/01-definition.md:L60`).
2. *Macro-Exposure Gap Vulnerability:* The premise that a negative gap between national readiness score and structural AI adoption exposure implies acute economic or societal vulnerability.
3. *Workforce Cognitive Deskilling:* The claim that long-term generative AI offloading causes lasting atrophy of analytical faculties (cataloged in `master-evidence-table.csv:L11` as `[UNVERIFIED — NEEDS SOURCE]`).

### 7.5 Category 5: Unlocated Items
- No unlocated statements were identified during this audit. Every assertion regarding HSRI's architecture, data, and pilots maps directly to local repository files. Any external concept not found in the codebase will be explicitly flagged as `UNLOCATED`.
