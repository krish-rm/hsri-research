# International Test Commission (ITC) Cultural and Linguistic Adaptation Guidelines

> **Protocol ID:** HSRI-ITC-2026-V1  
> **Applies to:** HSRI Standardized Behavioral Task Battery EXP-01 through EXP-09  
> **Standard:** Adheres to the International Test Commission (ITC) Guidelines for Translating and Adapting Tests (Second Edition, 2017)  
> **Version:** 1.0.0  

---

## 1. Objective and Guiding Principles

The objective of cultural adaptation is **construct equivalence**, not literal word-for-word translation. When porting behavioral task stimuli across languages (e.g., English, German, French, Japanese, Korean, Hindi, Portuguese):
1. The **cognitive trap** (e.g., fabricated citation, collider stratification bias, race condition, DCF double-counting) must remain logically identical.
2. The **domain plausibility** must fit local institutional contexts without altering problem difficulty ($\\beta$).
3. The **distractor attractiveness** must preserve balanced choice distributions.

---

## 2. Standard Forward-Backward Translation Protocol

For each target language edition:
1. **Stage 1 (Independent Forward Translations):** Two independent bilingual domain experts (e.g., native Japanese legal scholar and computational linguist) produce separate forward translations ($T_1, T_2$).
2. **Stage 2 (Synthesis & Reconciliation):** A reconciliation panel reconciles discrepancies to create Unified Draft $T_{syn}$.
3. **Stage 3 (Blind Backward Translation):** An independent native English speaker with no prior exposure to HSRI translates $T_{syn}$ back into English ($B_1$).
4. **Stage 4 (Psychometric Equivalency Review):** The principal psychometric committee compares $B_1$ with the original master English stimuli. Items showing semantic divergence or unintended cognitive cues are revised.
5. **Stage 5 (Cognitive Debriefing Pilot):** Piloted on $n=15$ native speakers per language using think-aloud protocols to confirm that error interception mechanisms are preserved.

---

## 3. Domain-Specific Cultural Adaptation Directives

### 3.1 Legal Scenarios (EXP-01)
- **Common Law Editions (US, UK, India, Canada, Australia):** Uses appellate case precedents, Shepard's citation conventions, and federal/provincial statutory provisions.
- **Civil Law Editions (Continental Europe, Japan, Latin America):** Replaces fictional court precedents with fabricated statutory cross-references (e.g., fictitious paragraphs in the Civil Code / BGB / Code Civil) maintaining identical logical contradiction structure.

### 3.2 Clinical Scenarios (EXP-02)
- Replaces US FDA-specific trade names with **International Nonproprietary Names (INN / WHO)** (e.g., *clarithromycin* and *simvastatin*) to prevent brand recognition artifacts.
- Ensures dosage ranges align with international pharmacopeia standards.

### 3.3 Technical Systems Scenarios (EXP-03)
- Software code snippets (Go/Rust/Python multi-threaded memory models) remain in standard English syntax across all language editions, as English is the universal lingua franca in software engineering.
- Problem prompts and diagnostic options are rendered in native languages.

### 3.4 Quantitative & Financial Scenarios (EXP-04)
- Currencies and units are localized where appropriate ($USD, EUR, JPY, INR$), but percentage capital structures (60/40), beta coefficients (1.15), and discount rates remain mathematically identical.
